Dictionary cache

A word is fetched from the network once and then served from this device. The cache is kept in chrome.storage.local, which has a 10 MB quota that this extension does not extend, so it holds at most 800 words and drops the oldest 200 when it overflows.

Sources, priority and licences

Sources are tried in this order and the first one that has an entry wins. The detail panel always names the source it used, so you can judge reliability.

  1. Free Dictionary API
    Primary dictionaryapi.dev Data derived from Wiktionary, CC BY-SA 3.0

    Returns phonetics, audio, definitions and examples, with no key. Community run, so a 5xx is retried once and then the next source is tried.

  2. Wiktionary REST API
    Fallback en.wiktionary.org CC BY-SA 3.0, attribution required

    Covers inflected and technical words the primary source lacks. Requests are spaced out and identify the client, as Wikimedia asks. Attribution appears in the detail panel and in every exported file.

  3. Datamuse
    Gap filler api.datamuse.com WordNet 3.0 glosses

    Used only when the two dictionaries have nothing. Returns short glosses and an ARPAbet pronunciation hint, and never example sentences, which is why it is not the primary source and why its phonetics are labelled as a hint.

  4. WordNet 3.0, local subset
    Offline, optional wordnet.princeton.edu Permissive licence, redistribution allowed

    Read from IndexedDB on demand. No key, no network, and the licence text travels with the data in data/wordnet-LICENSE.txt.

Wiktionary text is available under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported licence and is used here with attribution to Wiktionary contributors. WordNet 3.0 is distributed by Princeton University under a permissive licence that allows redistribution. No source returns non-English definitions, and no Chinese or English-Chinese dictionary is bundled or queried.

Privacy

What leaves this device, and what does not.

  • Page text never leaves your device. The content script reads the visible text in the tab you clicked the button on and turns it into a token list. Counting and ranking happen on the results page, locally.
  • The scan is stored only in chrome.storage.local, on this machine, and is replaced by the next scan. Nothing is synced to a Google account, because the extension does not request the sync scope.
  • The only outbound requests are dictionary lookups for a word you explicitly opened in the detail panel, or for the words you chose to export. A scan on its own contacts nothing.
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no remote code. The extension declares exactly three dictionary hosts in its manifest and loads no script or font from the network.
  • Dictionary lookups send the word alone. Nothing about the page, the URL, or the surrounding sentence is attached to the request.

Page content is never collected or uploaded. All counting happens locally in your browser.
